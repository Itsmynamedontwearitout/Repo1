# Fern

- **Species:** unknown. The nursery label only says "Fern". It has rusty-brown hairy stems, light-green finely divided fronds, and grows from a central crown. It may be a shield fern (*Polystichum*), but that's unconfirmed.
- **Usual spot:** indoors, raised on a box beside a large window.
- **Current spot:** on the balcony against a wall, away from other plants (quarantine).
- **Pot:** green glazed ceramic pot with a saucer.
- **Monitoring:** Willow sensor and app. If the sensor stayed inside, its readings don't reflect the balcony.
- **Label care:** indoors, bright indirect or filtered light; outdoors, part shade in a protected spot. Moist, humus-rich, well-drained soil. Water regularly.

## Status (2026-10-09)

Cut back hard after a heavy mealybug infestation. About 10 healthy, bright-green fronds remain, and a new fiddlehead is coming up from the crown. A few bits of white fluff are probably still hidden on the stems; one was visible on a main stem in the latest close-up.

## Next checks

Use no chemicals (see [preferences](../preferences.md)). At each check, look at the crown, the base of the stems, along the midribs, under the fronds and at new fiddleheads. Remove any fluff with tweezers or a cotton bud dipped in isopropyl. Full method: [mealybugs guide](../guides/mealybugs.md).

**Also watch for:**

- crisping from afternoon sun or wind on the balcony. If it happens, move the fern into more shade.

## Log

### 2026-10-09: mealybug infestation

- **Seen:** sticky, white "furry" growth on the stems, along the midribs and in the crown; brown, curled lower fronds. Willow reported conditions as fine.
- **Diagnosis:** mealybugs. The cottony bits are the insects in their wax coating, and the stickiness is their honeydew. Willow can't detect pests.
- **Done by Karl:** cut back most of the infested fronds, removed visible bugs with tweezers, sprayed isopropyl on the remaining fluff, and moved the fern to the balcony away from the succulent.
- **Outcome so far:** the plant looks healthy and is putting up new growth. Some bugs are probably still left.
- **Lesson:** Claude's first answer suggested a systemic insecticide, which Karl doesn't want. Lead with non-chemical options.
